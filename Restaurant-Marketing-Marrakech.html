Restaurant Marketing in Marrakech: 9 Ways to Get More Direct Reservations

A practical guide for Marrakech restaurant owners. Learn how restaurant marketing in Marrakech works in 2026 and how to win bookings on Google, Google Maps and WhatsApp without paying commission to intermediaries.

Short answer: to get more direct reservations in Marrakech, make sure tourists can find you on Google and Google Maps, trust you through reviews, read your menu online, and book in one tap through WhatsApp or your website. Instagram creates desire, but search is where booking decisions happen.

Marrakech has thousands of places to eat, from rooftop terraces overlooking Jemaa el-Fna to modern restaurants in Guéliz and Hivernage. Visitors arrive with a phone in hand and a simple question: where should we eat tonight? Whichever restaurants Google answers with win the table. If your business is missing from that answer, you are paying for it in empty seats, or in commissions to platforms that stand between you and your guests.

1. Why Marrakech Restaurants Need More Than Instagram

Instagram is excellent for atmosphere: plating, decor, sunsets over the Atlas. But it is a discovery channel, not a booking channel. Most followers scroll past, and few of them are in Marrakech on the right night.

Travellers who are ready to eat do something different. They search phrases like "restaurant near me", "best rooftop restaurant Marrakech" or "dinner Guéliz", and they choose from what Google Maps and the first results show. Those searches are high intent, local and often made minutes before booking.

Instagram makes people want your restaurant. Google decides whether they can find it, trust it and book it.

The goal is not to abandon social media. It is to add the channels that capture demand that already exists: your website, your Google Business Profile, your reviews and a frictionless way to book.

2. How to Optimise a Restaurant Website for Google

A restaurant website in Marrakech is not a brochure. It is your best salesperson, working 24 hours a day in several languages. To rank and convert, it needs to do a few things well:

  • Load fast on mobile. Most tourists search on 4G or hotel Wi-Fi. Compress images and avoid heavy sliders.
  • Say what and where you are, clearly. Put the cuisine, neighbourhood and city in your title tag and H1, for example "Moroccan Rooftop Restaurant in Marrakech Medina".
  • Show name, address and phone number consistently on every page, matching your Google listing exactly.
  • Add a booking action above the fold: WhatsApp button, call button or booking form.
  • Use structured data (Restaurant schema) so Google understands your hours, cuisine, price range and menu.
Quick win: search your restaurant name on your phone. If your website is slow, unreadable or missing, a guest is already choosing somebody else.

3. How to Appear in Google Maps Searches

Google Maps marketing for restaurants is often the single highest-return activity, because the map pack appears above regular results for local food searches. Google weighs relevance, distance and prominence, and you can influence all three.

  1. Claim and verify your Google Business Profile.
  2. Choose precise categories: a primary one such as "Moroccan restaurant", plus secondary ones like "Rooftop restaurant" or "Vegetarian restaurant" if true.
  3. Complete every field: opening hours, phone, website, attributes, menu link and booking link.
  4. Upload real photos weekly: dishes, interior, terrace, exterior and the entrance. In Marrakech, guests often struggle to find places in the Medina, so add photos of the route.
  5. Post updates about seasonal menus, events and live music.
  6. Answer questions and keep special hours updated for Ramadan and holidays.

Restaurants in Gueliz, Hivernage, the Medina, Palmeraie and Sidi Ghanem compete very differently. Your profile should signal your specific neighbourhood so Google can match you with the right nearby searches.

4. Why Every Restaurant Needs an Online Menu

Guests check the menu and prices before they book. If they cannot find one, they move on. A PDF or a photo of a printed menu is better than nothing, but a real web page menu is better still, because Google can read it and rank it.

  • Publish the menu as text on a page, not only as an image.
  • Include prices in MAD and, if relevant, a note on currency for international guests.
  • Mark vegetarian, vegan, halal and gluten-free options. Tourists search for these directly.
  • Offer English and French versions, and Arabic if your clientele includes local guests.
  • Link the menu from your Google Business Profile.

5. How to Collect More Google Reviews

Reviews are the trust signal that decides between two similar restaurants. They also feed Google's local ranking, so a steady flow of fresh, genuine reviews improves both visibility and conversion.

  • Make it effortless. Put a QR code on the bill, table or receipt linking straight to your Google review form.
  • Ask at the right moment: when a guest compliments the food, not when they are rushing out.
  • Train the team with one simple sentence they can say in English and French.
  • Reply to every review in the language it was written, including negative ones, calmly and specifically.
  • Never buy or fake reviews. It violates Google's policies and can get your listing suspended.
Target: aim for a consistent monthly flow of new reviews rather than a big burst. Regular activity looks healthier to both Google and readers.

6. WhatsApp Reservation Buttons and Click-to-Call

In Morocco, WhatsApp is how people communicate. Tourists also use it heavily while travelling because it works on Wi-Fi and avoids roaming costs. A WhatsApp booking button removes the biggest obstacle to a reservation: friction.

  • Add a WhatsApp button and a call button to your website, Google Business Profile and Instagram bio.
  • Pre-fill the message, for example: "Hello, I would like to book a table for 2 tonight at 8pm."
  • Reply quickly, ideally within minutes during service hours, and confirm by message.
  • Use WhatsApp Business with a greeting, away message and saved replies for menu and directions.

8. How to Reduce Dependence on Delivery Platforms and Booking Intermediaries

Platforms and intermediaries can bring guests, and that has value. The risk is dependence: you pay commission on each booking, you do not own the customer relationship, and your ranking can change overnight.

  • Measure the split. Track how many bookings come from intermediaries versus direct channels.
  • Steer repeat guests direct. Give a reason to book on your website or WhatsApp, such as a welcome drink or priority seating.
  • Capture contacts (with permission) for events, seasonal menus and special offers.
  • Own your ranking. Your own site and Maps listing keep working even if a platform changes its rules.

The aim is a healthier mix, not a boycott: more direct reservations, less commission, more control.
